# Engine notes

How the sim in `public/bruins-vs-canadiens/` is put together, what must stay true, and what we learned tuning it. `docs/MODELING.md` describes the same engine as a model (state, kinematics, policy, outcome models, calibration, inference) and what tracking data would change. Function names below are the ones in the code; "Where things live" names the file for each.

## File structure

`index.html` is the page markup (a header with the pitch and the view switcher; the Watch view with the strip that explains the lab, the scoreboard, canvas, control row, both teams' game plans and folded box score, play by play and rosters; the Game plan lab view; the Watch view's game-plan and settings drawers; About dialog; version footer), with `style.css` and one module, `src/main.js`, both loaded with a `?v=` version stamp. `src/engine/` is the engine. `sim.js` holds `createSim(DATA, opts)`: it builds the game state, installs the modules, and returns the state (`teams`, `game`, `puck`, `cfg`), `tick`, `newGame(seed)`, `setTactics`, `teamSample` and the helpers the page draws with. `rink.js` (geometry), `xg.js` (expected goals), `rosters.js` (ratings: `buildTeams`, which `newGame` calls again for every game), `rng.js` (the seeded generator) and `detmath.js` (the engine's math) are plain functions; Play Review imports `xg.js`, `rink.js` and `detmath.js` too. `officials.js`, `lines.js`, `formations.js`, `ai.js`, `physics.js` and `rules.js` each export `install(S)`: it takes the shared state and data from one object, `S`, adds its own functions to `S`, and returns a `link()` that fetches the other modules' functions once every module is installed, so the modules can call each other in any direction and the code inside each reads as it did in one file. The engine never touches the page; it emits `log` (a play-by-play line), `fx` (`ring`, `save`, `goal`), `shot` (at every release, before any block: the shooter, why he shot and the shot's xG), `final` and `reset`. `src/main.js` is the page inside `startSim(DATA)`: it creates the sim, listens to its events, draws it (the effects' timers, `fxTick`, run in the drawing loop), and runs the Watch controls, its game plans and settings, and the Game plan lab, with a small async loader at the end that fetches `data/*.json` and calls `startSim`. `src/lab/worker.js` is the lab's background worker: it imports the engine with the same stamp and plays seeded games. `docs/RESTRUCTURE.md` tells how the single file became these modules.

`startSim` exposes `window.rinkSim = { sim, tick, teams, game, puck, DATA, cfg, newGame, teamSample, runJobs, TEST }` for the headless checks. `tick(dt)` advances the game by `dt` seconds (the frame loop uses 0.03 s substeps); the page checks drive it directly with the drawing loop paused. The engine checks do not need the page at all: `tools/engine.js` loads `createSim` in Node and sets it up the way a fresh visit to the page does.

## Coordinates

The rink is 200 by 85 feet in canvas units, goal lines at x = 11 and 189, blue lines at 75 and 125. Above the ice is a 12 ft bench band (`BENCH_H`) and below it a 10 ft penalty-box band; `RINK_Y` offsets the ice. Everything in the engine is in feet.

Formations and most rules use team-relative coordinates: `toFx(t, x)` gives distance from the team's own goal line (0) toward the far goal line (178), `toRink(t, fx, fy)` converts back, `zone(t, x)` returns `own` (fx < 64), `neutral` or `off` (fx > 114). `t.dir` is +1 for a team attacking right and flips every period (`endPeriod`), so anything that depends on direction must go through these helpers or `t.dir`, never a raw x.

## Where things live

Each section names its file in `src/engine/` (or `src/main.js` for the page).

**Rosters and ratings** (`rosters.js`: `buildRosters`, `mkSkater`, `mkGoalie`). Players are built from `rosters.json` with ratings from `moneypuck-2025.json` when the player has enough minutes, otherwise from the stat-line formulas in `ratings.json`. Skating speed is `27 + skate * 6` ft/s top speed (NHL EDGE), acceleration from `physics.json`. Goalies carry save percentage by danger tier and a freeze tendency.

**Game state and officials** (`sim.js`: `newGame`, `tick`; `officials.js`: `blowWhistle`, `moveOfficials`; `lines.js`: `setFaceoff`). `game.faceoff` is set during a stoppage; `game.faceT` counts down the pause. Three officials follow NHL lead/trail mechanics and are drawn but never touch the play.

**Lines, benches and shifts** (`lines.js`: `pattern`, `nextLine`, `buildUnit`, `stoppageChange`, `matchLines`, `safeToChange`, `defending`, `shiftTarget`, `startUnitChange`, `startSoloChange`, `manageChanges`, `benchMinor`). Rotation patterns and shift targets come from `systems.json` deployment. Each bench has two doors (`fDoor`, `dDoor`, `doorFor`): forwards use the door at the attacking end, defense the other, so they swap with `t.dir`. Change windows are in `SYS.deployment.changeWindows`; the only on-the-fly windows are our carrier through the neutral zone, or the opponent retrieving deep in his own end. Nobody changes off a loose puck or while defending. Past `desperateAfterSec` a unit takes the first half-decent window and its carrier clears early.

**Formations** (`formations.js`: `formationSpot`). One function returns the spot for a player given situation (offense, defense, loose) and zone. Offense in own zone starts at the breakout spots and, once possession has lasted `breakout.flow.startAfterSec`, moves the forwards north ahead of the puck. Neutral-zone offense is relative to the puck (lead winger, strong-side winger, center trailer, D trailing). Offensive zone uses the chosen system's spots, with the cycle rotating its three forward spots every `offensiveZone.motion.cycleEverySec` and a small drift on every spot. Defense: forecheck spots in their zone, puck-relative spots in neutral (shorthanded, the penalty kill's own passive forecheck, `penaltyKill.forecheck`: one man pressures, the rest hold the middle and our blue line), and in our zone either the penalty-kill slots, man coverage, or zone coverage where the center owns the slot, D box out the net-front man (a defenseman who has come down counts for both since sim 0.61; before, only forwards did) (or stand `zone.dSpread` off the middle), wingers shade the high man, and any attacker in the slot gets the nearest free defender first (`zone.slotCoverage`, sticky). Man coverage (`manMarks`, once per tick) marks every attacker including the carrier; the man on the carrier is the one who pressures him, marks switch only past `man.stickFt`, and each defender plays between his man and the net with a cushion that grows with the man's distance from it. In our zone, in both systems, the man on the carrier plays him from the inside (in the middle of the ice; on the wall or below the goal line he closes to `defensiveZone.carrierPressure.wallGoalSideFt`, still inside, to take the puck, since sim 0.62: before that he held a stick length off a carrier who was no threat, nobody ever took the puck from him, and pinned shifts ran past three minutes), `man.carrierGoalSideFt` or `zone.carrierGoalSideFt` (a stick length) between the carrier and the net, rather than skating at the puck (man since sim 0.58, zone since 0.59; before it, man coverage had nobody goal-side of the carrier, since it has no shot fronter, and gave up about 2 expected goals a game more than zone). In our own zone a defender more than `skater.effort.defendOwnZoneUrgentBeyondFt` from his spot skates at full effort. Gap control caps a defenseman's depth at the deepest attacker minus `gapControl.gapFt`, reading the attacker's speed `gapControl.readAheadSec` ahead. Angling (`ai.js` `angleOff`, `physics.json` `angling`, since sim 0.56): from just before the blue line, a defender goal-side of the carrier and in his way bends the carrier's route to the outside of him, so a carrier can no longer skate through a defenseman into the slot; it changes where he skates, not what he decides.

**AI** (`ai.js`: `assignTargets`, `carrierThink`, `pass`, `shoot`, `chip`, `clearPuck`, `laneClear`, `shotQuality`, `leadForward`). `assignTargets` picks the chaser (nearest to the puck or carrier; a second chaser on 2-1-2 and lock forechecks), a fronter in our zone, the dump-and-chase sprinter, and gives everyone else a formation spot, clamped onside at fx 106 while we attack. `carrierThink` runs the carrier's decision each tick: in the offensive zone, shoot or pass by expected goals (`lookXg`, `shotDecision.xgChoice`: his look against the best pass, discounted for a defender in the lane or on him), then perimeter shots through traffic, probabilistic and point shots; breakaway, spring pass to a lead forward, the chosen zone entry (headman, carry, chip, dump), the stuck rule after `shotDecision.stuckAfterSec`, on the penalty kill in our zone or the neutral zone a clear the length of the ice within a beat of getting the puck (`pkClear`, `penaltyKill.clear`: the open lane nearest straight ahead, a lift over the sticks when every lane is covered; only a breakaway or an odd-man rush up the neutral zone keeps it), clearing under pressure in our zone, and the open-man pass (`pass.want`; in the offensive zone only after `pass.controlSec` with the puck). A pass on its way to a teammate counts as that team's puck in `assignTargets` (`inFlight`): both sides keep their structure and the defense plays the receiver. Defenders get one stick on a pass that goes by within `pass.stickReachFt`, and an uncontrolled touch knocks it off line.

**Expected goals** (`xg.js`: `xgModel`, `xgTier`, `XGF`). The logistic model from `xg-model.json`, evaluated in `shoot()` with the shot's distance, angle, type, rebound and strength; `puck.xg` feeds the team total and the goalie's save roll. `docs/XG-MODEL.md` has the fit and the calibration story.

**Movement and physics** (`physics.js`: `movePlayers`, `fatigueLevel`, `fatigue`, `hands`, `movePuck`, `keepOffNets`, `keepOnIce`, goalie helpers). Effort: full speed when chasing, rushing, bursting on a breakout, back-checking caught above the puck, or far from the spot; otherwise cruise. Fatigue scales top speed and acceleration down and pass and shot error up after `skater.fatigue.startsAtShiftSec`. The puck has friction, board and rim restitution, and `puck.cool` prevents instant re-pickup after releases. `movePuck` handles pickups, steals, blocks (`shotBlock`), goalie saves against the goalie line (`goalieLine`, one save roll per shot via `puck.beaten`), rebounds kicked to the corner, freezes, goals, icing and the dead-puck whistle.

**Rules** (`rules.js`: `checkOffside`, `whistleOffside`, `penalize`, `releaseFromBox`, hybrid icing inside `movePuck` in `physics.js`). Tag-up offside with a delayed flag, hybrid icing judged at the dots with the icing team unable to change, penalties drawn from each player's MoneyPuck penalty rate, too-many-men on a mid-change turnover.

**Effects** (`src/main.js`: `FX`, `fxTick`, `drawFx`; the engine emits `fx` events for them). Skate trails, puck streak, rings on takeaways and blocks, save flash, goal light, scorer ring. All drawn under the players and cleared at every whistle. Two toggles, Effects and Sticks, remembered in `localStorage`.

**Review a game** (`src/review.js`: `installReview`, installed by `src/main.js` with the drawing functions it shares; its own view since sim 0.68). A recorder (`makeRecorder(E, onFinal)`) listens only, to one engine: on each `shot` event it stores the shooter's `readLook` at the release and the play's last `review.leadSec` seconds (sampled `review.sampleHz` times a second after every tick, `step`), then `review.afterSec` more; a goal settles the scoring team's latest shot (the engine counts a shot on goal when it beats the goalie, a few frames before the line). `judge` sorts each shot into better pass missed, close call and right call; at `final` it hands over the finished game (`moments`, team names, final score) and `reset` starts afresh. The replay draws the recorded frames on its own canvas with `drawRink`, `drawSkater` and `paintSight` through `withCtx`, zoomed to the ice the play used (`viewOf`). One recorder is on the Watch rink. A lab game opened with Review it is played on an engine of its own (`playRecipe`: the recipe is the seed, both teams' tactics and the NHL settings; `tick(0.03)` and the recorder's `step`, 4,000 ticks a frame), and its final is checked against the lab's row. Recent games (`H`): up to 8 entries, lab ones saved as recipes in `localStorage` (`rink.reviews`, for this sim version), Watch ones only for the visit, since a Watch game ticks with the frame rate and its plans can change mid-game, so it cannot be played again exactly. A recording is about 10 MB (the frames around each shot, and a snapshot per shot), so only 4 keep theirs: the one on screen, then Watch games, then the latest lab games; a lab game without one is played again when reopened. The lab's own games in the workers are never recorded. Print or save as PDF (sim 0.69): `report()` builds the game's report (a still of every better pass missed and every goal, drawn with `paint` on the replay canvas and copied onto white, then every shot and every shooter) and `main.js` `labReport()` the lab's (baseline plans, record, the comparison with the real teams, every result card through `paperCard`). `printReport` puts it in `#printOut` and calls the browser's `print()`; the print styles show only `#printOut`, in the light colors whatever the theme. `beforeprint` builds the same report when someone prints from the browser's menu; `afterprint` clears it. No PDF is made by the page. What if he passed: every `review.snapshotEverySec` of play `step` keeps `sim.snapshot()` and the tick lengths since; a shot keeps the latest with its ticks (`m.replay`). On demand, a second engine (`kit.makeSim`) restores it and replays those ticks to the instant before the release (`rebuild`, which checks the shooter stands where he did and has the puck), snapshots that instant, and plays `runOne` for the pass and the shot `whatIf.runs` times each, forcing the choice with `force` and pairing seeds with `reseed`. The verdict is the paired 95% range of net chances; one typical pass run is replayed with frames for If he passed. Engine side: `snapshot`, `restore`, `reseed`, `force` in `sim.js` (`copyInto` copies the state graph, sharing data and keeping the root objects' identity) and the forced-decision check at the top of `carrierThink`.

**Drawing and HUD** (`src/main.js`: `drawRink`, `drawBenches`, `drawPlayers`, `drawSkater`, `drawGoalie`, `drawOfficials`, `drawWhistle`, `updateHud`, roster sheets, `renderCalib`, `drawSight`, `paintSight`, `withCtx`). The sight lines (`drawSight`, toggled with `FX.sight`, painted by `paintSight`, which the shot review shares) draw the carrier's read in the offensive zone from the same `readLook` that `carrierThink` decides with: a lit wedge to the posts, a shadow behind each defender in front of him, a dashed line to the best pass with the receiver ringed, and a tag with both values and the carrier's read (shooting, passing or holding) by the same thresholds `carrierThink` uses. They read the game and change nothing. The reality check (`renderCalib`, `REAL_ROWS`, folded under the lab's baseline, grouped into results, scoring, shot quality, defense and game flow, each row with a plain reading and an on target / above / below verdict against that team's real number, within 15% is on target) reads `teamSample` from each baseline game and compares with `moneypuck-2025.json` benchmarks, and shows the baseline's record and points percentage against each club's final record from `rosters.json`.

**Game end** (`rules.js`: `endPeriod`, `shootout`; `src/main.js`: `recordResult`, on the engine's `final` event). A game tied after three goes to a five-minute overtime (`game.period === 4`, full strength) and then `shootout()`: three shooters a side, best shooters first, then sudden death; a shooter's chance is `game.shootout.leagueRate` in `physics.json` scaled by his shoot rating and the goalie's high-danger save rate relative to the league. `game.overtime` and `game.shootout` mark how the game ended, `recordResult` adds it once (`game.recorded`) to the session `record` shown on the Game tab, and `newGame` resets the flags.

**Views and drawers** (`src/main.js`: `showView`, `openDrawer`, `renderPlans`, `TERMS`). `showView` switches Watch and the Game plan lab; the page opens on Watch, and `#lab` (or the old `#test` and `#real`) opens the lab. `TERMS` holds the plain explanation of every term: a ? button with `data-term` shows one, and the How it works dialog lists them all as a glossary. The game-plan drawer belongs to Watch: it holds both teams' selects (the ids `tools/engine.js` reads the options from), each with its description from `systems.json` under it, and only its controls feed `readTactics`; `renderPlans` writes each plan as a sentence on the Watch view. The settings drawer (the sliders) also belongs to Watch.

**Game plan lab** (`src/main.js`, `src/lab/worker.js` and `src/lab/read.js`, which holds the averages, 95% ranges, verdicts, the baseline's headline and the order of a test's games, with no page in it so `tools/test/lab.test.js` can check them: `LAB`, `TEST`, `buildLab`, `labRefresh`, `renderBaseline`, `renderCalib`, `compare`, `playOnBase`, `diffs`, `runJobs`, `runInPage`, `makePool`, the run, test and rank handlers). It has its own plans for both teams (`LAB.plans`; the originals are `defaults.json`'s systems) and never touches the Watch game, except that Watch these plans in a game copies them into the plan drawer and starts a new game. The page reads top to bottom: the baseline card, both plans side by side, the buttons, the tests. The baseline (`LAB.base`) is both plans over seeds 1 to n, with a copy of the plans it was played with. A test plays the plans as they are now (either team, any number of changes, listed by `diffs`) on the baseline's seeds and compares game for game from the coached team's side (`compare`); a ranking (Find the best option, `rankOptions`, `rankCard`) plays every option of one choice in the baseline plan, states the best option or that none separates, and can put a clear winner into `LAB.plans`. Results stack in `LAB.tested` (a test keeps only its own rows and the baseline indexes they pair with). Rerun is enabled when the plans differ from the baseline (or the games count changed) and Test when they changed since the last test (`LAB.lastRun`) and differ from the baseline; rerunning clears the tests. Plans, baseline, tests and `lastRun` are saved in `localStorage` as `rink.lab` with the sim version; results saved by another version are dropped, plans kept. A run is a list of jobs, one per game: a seed, both teams' tactics and the engine multipliers, always the NHL-average tick. `runJobs` plays them in background workers, one per spare core up to eight (`makePool`): each worker imports the engine (`src/lab/worker.js`), receives `DATA` once, and plays each job as `tools/lab.js` would, with `newGame(seed)`. If a browser cannot start workers, `runInPage` plays the same jobs on the page, 4,000 ticks a frame, on an engine instance of its own, so the game being watched is only paused. Because a change plays the baseline's seeds, the verdicts compare game for game: net chances (chances for minus against), goal difference and points, each a mean with a 95% interval, labeled clear edge, too close to call or clear cost by whether the interval excludes zero. The baseline's headline leads with chances, judged the same way against even, and its points share against half the points. The baseline's games are the only input to the comparison with the real teams (`renderCalib`); the Watch game is never graded. The lab has no engine logic of its own; a seeded game plays identically in a worker, on the page and in `tools/lab.js` (`lab.js --pool` checks it), and in every browser (`tools/browsers.js`).

The aggression dial (`t.tac.agg`, 0 to 1) feeds exactly four things: the steal rate and the minor-penalty rate when a checker is on the carrier (`turnover.*PerAggression`), the probabilistic shot rate, and how long the carrier holds the puck under pressure. Its description, and every system's, lives in `systems.json` and is shown as hover text and in the About dialog.

## Invariants

- Only the helpers above read direction. If something looks mirrored in the second period, a raw x slipped in.
- Every puck that crosses the goal line between the posts, whoever touched it last, has beaten a save roll; a unit test fails otherwise. Until sim 0.57 a puck last touched by the defending team (a rebound drifting back, a deflection or block off a defender) went in with no roll: about three goals in ten.
- Each shot gets exactly one save roll (`puck.beaten`), and whether it is on net is decided at release (`puck.onNet`). Every puck that reaches the goal line between the posts gets that roll, including one that slipped past where the goalie stood; before 0.35 those went in without one (about two goals per team per game at the 0.35 settings). Wide pucks the goalie touches are misses, not saves. The save roll divides xG by the league on-net share of the shot's danger tier because xG is per unblocked attempt and the roll only sees pucks that reached the goalie; `shoot()` puts shots on net at those same rates (`shot.aim`, since sim 0.60), so the division is exact.
- A game depends only on its seed. `newGame(seed)` gives it its own generator (`rng.js`; every draw goes through `random()` or `rnd()`), rebuilds both teams, the puck and the clock from scratch (`buildTeams`, `freshPuck`, `freshGame`), and picks the opening lines only after that (the rotation reads the clock, the period, the score and both penalty boxes). `node --test tools/test` checks all three. Check with `tools/lab.js --workers 1` against `--workers 3`: the rows must be identical.
- A player is never in two places: `onIce`, `leaving`, `entering`, `box` are mutually consistent; `rebuildOnIce` recomputes `t.players` after any change.
- Formation spots are clamped to the ice and attackers are held onside at fx 106 until the puck crosses the far blue line.
- `tick` must never throw. `tools/smoke.js` fails the check on any page error.
- The engine's math is exact or comes from `detmath.js`; never `Math.sin`, `cos`, `atan2`, `exp`, `log`, `pow`, `hypot` or `**`, which differ in the last bit between browsers and Node.

## Baselines (sim 0.62 references)

Measure with `tools/lab.js`: `node tools/lab.js --nhl` and `node tools/lab.js`, 100 games each from seed 1, eight at a time in Node. Each game plays its own seed (`newGame(seed)`), and the engine's trigonometry and logarithms come from `detmath.js`, so a run reproduces exactly, in Node and in any browser, and the report says "identical to the reference" until the engine changes. The references are in `tools/baselines.json`; re-save with `--save` only for an intended change. Each reference also keeps a fingerprint of every game, and `--order` replays the seeds in reverse order in one process to prove no state leaks between games, and `--pool` plays them in the page's own workers to prove the page and Node agree. To try a data value without editing the file: `--param shotDecision.xgChoice.minXg=0.14`. In the report "unblocked attempts" is a club's attempts minus the blocks against them (the engine's `blocked` counts blocks a team made, `blockedOwn` shots its own skaters caught in flight); it equals the three danger tiers added up.

At the NHL-average tick, per team per game, against the league (MoneyPuck 2025-26):

| | League | BOS | MTL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goals | 3.08 | 3.24 | 2.92 |
| Expected goals | 3.13 | 3.89 | 4.23 |
| Shots on goal | 27.8 | 24.1 | 33.4 |
| Unblocked attempts | 42.7 | 38.0 | 53.2 |
| Low / medium / high danger | 31.2 / 8.1 / 3.4 | 20.9 / 13.6 / 3.5 | 36.9 / 13.1 / 3.2 |
| Offsides | 3.0 | 2.2 | 6.1 |
| Icings | 4.2 | 7.7 | 7.0 |

These are the references `tools/lab.js --nhl` compares against (`tools/baselines.json`), saved at sim 0.62 when the man on a carrier on the wall started closing on him. Boston takes 62% of the points and Montreal 46% (real 2025-26: 61% and 65%; head to head the two cannot both be above .500, and the interval is about ±10); Boston's share of expected goals is 48% (real 47%). Boston turns expected goals into goals better (0.83 against 0.69 a game), mostly Swayman's real medium-danger save rate, about 15% better than Dobes's, and noise of about ±0.3 goals a game.

Goals match the league because `goalie.tierGoalScale` is 1.02 (1.05 in sim 0.61, 0.92 in 0.60, 0.74 in 0.59, 0.68 in 0.58, 0.57 in 0.57; 0.39 from 0.35 to 0.56, while about three goals in ten went in with no save roll). Everything else is honest: since 0.35 a carrier takes every look worth taking, so the sim's chance count shows how many looks its defense gives up, which is 20 to 30% too many (60 to 90% before sim 0.58), mostly medium-danger looks off passes into the low slot: about 13 a team a game against 8; high-danger looks are at the league rate. The biggest single source, for both clubs, is a forward 10 to 20 ft out at even strength taking a pass and shooting on his read, about 5 a game each. Montreal shoots far more than the league from the point and is offside nearly twice the league rate; icings run high. At build defaults (acceleration 1.8x, speed 1.3x, shot volume 2x) goals are 3.7 and 3.6 a game on 4.6 and 5.2 expected, and the points split about evenly (Boston 57%, Montreal 49%); at the old 2.3x acceleration Montreal took three quarters of them, because every defender recovered almost at once and man coverage gained the most from it; Boston ices it about 9 times a game.

How 0.35 got here, measured each step with `lab.js --nhl` (both clubs' expected goals): the expected-goal decision alone took the sim from about 4 to 12 a game, because open looks were everywhere and players had been passing them up; passes no longer turning the defense into a loose-puck scramble, man coverage that keeps its marks with a cushion, and the pass control time brought it back to 5 to 6. Several sensible changes measured almost nothing on their own (slot pick-up, sticks on passes, own-zone effort, defenseman spacing) and were kept because they are right, not because they moved the number.

## System effects (sim 0.35)

Measured at sim 0.35. The engine's own math (0.43) and the dead-pass fix (0.46) changed every game a little, so before relying on a number here, re-run its sweep (`node tools/lab.js --nhl --sweep BOS.<card>`) and update the table.

The question the lab exists to answer: does changing a card move the result, by more than the noise, in a direction a hockey person would accept? Every Bruins card against the Canadiens' build cards, NHL-average tick, 100 games per option, the same seeds for every option (`node tools/lab.js --nhl --sweep BOS.<card>`). What it says:

- Records are still noise at these sample sizes: about ±12 points on a points-percentage difference at 100 games. Expected-goal differential separates at about ±0.4 a game; read it first.
- More of the effects now point the way analysts would expect: controlled entries (carry, headman) create about a goal's worth of expected goals more than dumping it in, chipping it creates less, and the penalty kill's diamond protects the slot better than chasing.
- Man-on-man gave up 2 expected goals a game more than zone, because nobody played the puck carrier from the inside. Fixed in sim 0.58. Since zone coverage also plays the carrier from the inside (0.59), zone gives up fewer expected goals than man for Montreal at the NHL settings: about 0.8 a game in 0.59, 0.5 since man coverage sags more in 0.61 (the slot pick-up and the shot fronter).
- Crashing the net still lowers expected goals for, and the umbrella flipped from better than the cycle to worse. Both offensive-zone systems need a look at where their forwards stand.
- Aggression no longer does anything measurable. It fed the old probabilistic shot rate and the reflex pass, which the expected-goal decision now outweighs; its steal and penalty effects are too small to show. It needs a real job (forecheck depth, pinching, how hard the chaser goes).
- The power-play systems still do nothing measurable.

### Lopsided games (sim 0.63)

An outside review of the Game plan lab found two 10-goal games for Montreal in 50 when the Bruins played man-on-man (lab seeds 11 and 19 at the NHL settings: 10-2 on 6.6 expected goals, 10-3 on 7.4). Measured on seeds 1 to 200 with the lab's default plans, the Bruins' defensive zone set to man and then to zone:

| Bruins coverage | MTL goals a game | MTL expected goals | Games with 6+ MTL expected goals | MTL 8+ goals | MTL 10+ goals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Man-on-man | 4.18 | 5.25 | 51 | 9 | 3 |
| Zone | 3.25 | 4.27 | 9 | 2 | 0 |

Nothing runs away inside a game. Play does not depend on the score except the late-game line changes, the save roll depends only on the shot and the goalie's tier save rates, and a team up by three or more finishes at its usual rate (Montreal 0.81 goals per expected goal while up 3+, 0.80 over all games under man). The 10-goal games are man-on-man giving up too many chances, the same excess as the defense item in `docs/ROADMAP.md`, on a night when the shots go in. Two things a real blowout has are missing: no starter is ever pulled for the backup (a real starter rarely faces a sixth or seventh goal), and there are no score effects (a trailing team pushing, a leading team sitting back), which in the NHL shrink a lead's chance share. A 10-goal night is a few a season across the league, about one team-game in a thousand; at 1.5% under man, the engine has about ten times too many, and none under zone in 200.

## Tuning knobs most likely to matter

`physics.json`: `shotDecision.*` (the expected-goal choice, rates per rule, breakaway detection), `goalie.tierGoalScale`, `goalie.freeze`, `shot.xg`, `skater.fatigue`, `skater.effort`, `turnover`, `pass.*`, `puck.friction`, `rulesBenchmarks`.
`systems.json`: `breakout.flow`, `offensiveZone.motion`, `entry.dump`, `clearing`, `deployment.shiftTargetsSec`, `deployment.changeWindows`, formation spots per system.
`defaults.json`: what the sliders and systems start at.

## Lessons learned

Things that were wrong once and the fix, so nobody reintroduces them.

- Lead forwards overshooting the blue line: braking distance was ignored; the onside clamp now sits at fx 106 and targets back off at 113.
- Offsides on faceoffs: the faceoff zone flag leaked into the offside check; clear it at the drop.
- No icings at all: puck friction was far too high; `puck.friction` is now 0.008 and clears aim past the far blue line.
- Defense pinned to the goal line: gap control applied in our own zone; it is now skipped there.
- Man coverage marking point men: D take forwards only (`manMark`).
- Goalie line swinging parallel to the shot: the line is squared from the net toward the puck, smoothed by `goalie.turnRate`, with a body-radius floor.
- Multiple save rolls per shot and rebound chains: one roll (`puck.beaten`), rebounds kicked to the corner with `reboundCoolSec`.
- Power-play freeze (everyone stopped): the stuck rule and a PP drift keep the carrier moving.
- Stuck passes thrown out of the zone: stuck-rule passes go only to deep teammates.
- Dump on every possession: a dump window (fx 92 to 108) plus a cooldown.
- Rim dying beside the net: dump speed 125 ft/s and rim restitution 0.93.
- Lines peeling off during offensive-zone possession: the loose-puck change window was the cause; removed, replaced by the two windows and fatigue.
- Forwards standing still on breakouts and in the cycle: formation spots were fixed; see `breakout.flow` and `offensiveZone.motion`.
- Gassed units icing the puck constantly: the gassed clear now needs pressure within `clearing.gassedPressureFt` (8 ft), not the penalty-kill distance.
- Hidden panes showing in the standalone file: `[hidden] { display: none !important; }` is required in the CSS.
- The xG model's unmeasured features (puck speed before the shot, off wing) must take their dataset means, or every shot is inflated by about a third. MoneyPuck's rush flag is empty in 2025-26 and must not be used.
- The old distance-box tiers hid a shot mix that was far too close to the net; the first xG swap tripled goals until the decision rates were retuned against the real tier split.
- Measuring pace: compare builds with the same harness and the same multipliers; a quick harness that skipped whistle ticks produced misleading averages once.
- Play frozen for a period (before 0.46): a pass that died just short of the far blue line stayed "in flight" because nothing checked that it was still moving. Its receiver was held onside at fx 106, the defense played him instead of the puck, and a defender standing within 6 ft kept the dead-puck whistle from blowing, so one line stayed out for up to 20 minutes, in about one game in ten at the build defaults. A pass now counts as in flight only above `pass.inFlightMinFtPerSec`. Letting the receiver skate up to the line also unfroze it, but it brought back the blue-line overshoot (offsides up by a goal's worth a game), so that half was dropped. `node --test tools/test` plays the two seeds that froze.
- Math that differs between engines: before 0.43 the engine used `Math.sin` and friends, and a seeded game played differently in Node than in Chromium (2 of 20 games at first), because browsers may round those in the last bit however they like. `detmath.js` fixed it for every engine.
- Sample size: ten games cannot separate two close strategies; at 50% the Wilson interval on 10 games is 24 to 76 points wide, on 100 games about 40 to 60. The lab prints the interval for that reason.
