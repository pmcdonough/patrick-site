# Modeling guide

Every model in this repo, what it is built on, how it was fit or tuned, and where the numbers live. Read this to know which parts are measured, which are calibrated, and which are inferred or mocked. After the table: a technical summary of the engine as a model, and what full tracking data would change. The other docs go deeper: `ENGINE.md` for the code, `XG-MODEL.md` for the expected-goal fit, `PLAY-REVIEW.md` for the review page. The sim's How it works dialog tells the same story for visitors.

## Three kinds of number

Measured numbers come straight from a source and can be checked against it: a player's save percentage by tier, the league's shots per game, a shot's coordinates. They are cited in the data file that holds them.

Calibrated numbers are chosen so the sim reproduces a measured number: skating speeds against EDGE, decision rates against the danger-tier mix, `tierGoalScale` against goals per game. Each one is in a data file with a description that says what it was tuned to, and `docs/ENGINE.md` records the baselines they produce.

Inferred and mocked numbers are the engine's or the page's own guesses where no data exists: where the other skaters stood at a real shot, and how everyone moved in the seconds around it. The review page labels every panel that shows one, and nothing inferred feeds back into a measured or calibrated number.

## Provenance rules

The raw inputs (MoneyPuck CSVs, NHL JSON) are not committed; the scripts that turn them into the compact JSON files are (`tools/mp_extract.py`, `tools/xg_fit.py`, `tools/game_load.py`), and the outputs carry a `sources` block with the file names, row counts and terms. Each script reproduces its committed output exactly from the inputs listed in `docs/RAW-INPUTS.md`. Refitting means re-running the script, never editing the numbers. The expected-goal formula is implemented once, `src/engine/xg.js`, and the sim and Play Review both import it and read the same `xg-model.json`.

## Known weaknesses, in order of how much they matter

1. The sim's shot mix has more medium-danger looks than the real game; the goal scale compensates and the reality check shows it. Fixing it means better defensive coverage, not a different scale.
2. The review page's inferred positions make the net-front pass look available more often than it is. Tracking data is the fix; until then the page says so on every panel.
3. The decision model decides between shooting and passing by the xG model since sim 0.35, but the volume rules (perimeter, point and probabilistic shots) still use a geometric shot quality, and aggression no longer moves anything measurable (`docs/ENGINE.md`, System effects).
4. The replay is a reconstruction for the eye, with no claim to accuracy beyond the real event locations and the real result.
5. Systems are drawn from coaching references, not from either club's observed play; a pre-scout product needs observed systems per opponent.
6. The Game plan lab compares strategies inside the model only. A difference between two rows is what the engine thinks, and the engine's system effects have not been validated against real results. The interval shows the model's own sampling noise, not its bias.

## The engine as a model, technically

**State and time.** A discrete-time simulation with a fixed step of 0.03 s of game time. The state is the position and velocity of 12 bodies on the ice plus the puck, the clock, the score, the penalty boxes, line rotation and fatigue (seconds on the current shift), and per-team flags for delayed offside, dumps and possession. A game is a deterministic function of its seed: every random draw comes from a per-game mulberry32 generator, and the transcendental functions come from `detmath.js`, so the trajectory is bit-identical in Node and in every browser.

**Kinematics.** Skaters are points steered toward a target with bounded acceleration and speed: top speed 27 + 6 x skate rating ft/s, acceleration 21 ft/s², both scaled by effort (cruise, sprint, burst) and a fatigue curve that starts at 30 s of shift and saturates at 65 s. Bodies are kept off the nets and inside the boards; skaters do not collide physically; contact is resolved as events (takeaways, blocks, stick deflections). The puck is a particle with per-frame friction and a restitution coefficient off the boards. Passes and shots leave at rating-dependent speeds (passes 55 to 85 ft/s, wrist shots 85 + 40 x shoot ft/s, slap shots 118 + 28 x shoot ft/s). A pass has a uniform angular aiming error whose width grows with distance, a weaker rating and fatigue. A shot is on net with the league's probability for its danger tier (MoneyPuck 2025-26, 63 / 69 / 78%) and then aimed inside the posts or just wide of one; since sim 0.60, when an aiming error like the pass's put close shots on net 95% of the time and point shots 30 to 50%. A goalie is a segment of rating-dependent half-width that turns toward the puck at a bounded rate and moves within a clamped crease area.

**Policy.** A hand-written, rule-based policy, not a learned one. Each team's system defines target spots in a team-relative frame (fx from the team's own goal line, fy across the ice), mirrored for the end it attacks and the puck side, with situation-specific motion (breakout flow, cycle rotation, rush lanes). Assignment is greedy nearest-first with hysteresis (a player keeps a mark unless another is closer by a margin): one or two chasers by forecheck, slot coverage and a shot fronter in zone defense, marks in man coverage, slots on the penalty kill. The carrier's choice each step compares option values: his own shot's expected goals times his finishing, discounted for a defender in the lane or on him; each pass, the receiver's expected goals times a completion probability from lane geometry times pressure; carrying, chipping, dumping or clearing by zone, system and pressure. Thresholds (minimum look worth shooting, the margin a pass must beat the shot by, control time before a discretionary pass) are data, with per-second hazard rates for the perimeter, point and probabilistic shot rules that produce the volume of low-danger attempts.

**Outcome models.** Expected goals: a logistic regression on distance, ln(distance + 1), distance², angle, angle², distance x angle, shot type, rebound, strength and two features the engine fills with dataset means (`XG-MODEL.md`). Save: one Bernoulli draw per puck that reaches the goalie, P(goal) = xG / on-net share of its tier x (goalie tier goal rate / league tier goal rate) x `tierGoalScale`. Steals and penalties are hazard rates per second of exposure, scaled by ratings, aggression and fatigue; blocks, deflections and freezes are probabilities per event (a shot through a defender, a pass past a stick, a save with traffic).

**Calibration and fit.** Measured inputs enter directly (ratings, tier save rates, the xG coefficients). Calibrated parameters are tuned one at a time against league aggregates at the NHL-average settings: goals, shots, attempts, the danger-tier mix, offsides, icings, shift lengths. There is no joint fit and no likelihood for the policy; the comparison with the real teams in the Game plan lab is the scorecard, and today about a quarter of its 28 numbers are on target. The chief miss, 20 to 30% more expected goals than the league (60 to 90% before sim 0.58), comes from coverage that allows too many medium-danger looks.

**Inference in the lab.** Strategy effects are estimated by paired Monte Carlo with common random numbers: the baseline and a test share seeds, so each game's difference removes most of the shared noise. Effects are reported as the mean paired difference with a 1.96 standard-error interval; a verdict is "clear" only when the interval excludes zero. Records need far more games than chances to separate, so net chances (xG for minus against) lead. The intervals describe the model's sampling noise only; they say nothing about whether the model's system effects match real hockey.

## What full tracking data would change

The NHL's puck and player tracking records the position of every skater, both goalies and the puck many times a second, for every game. Each model above has a specific use for it.

| Model | Today | With tracking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kinematics | Two numbers per skater, scaled from a rating against EDGE's published ranges | Per-player acceleration, top speed, turning and stopping profiles estimated from his own trajectories, by situation and fatigue |
| Decision policy | Hand-written rules and thresholds from coaching references | A discrete-choice model (shoot, pass to each teammate, carry, dump, chip) fit to observed choices given the full state at each decision; pass completion and interception fit to real lanes, distances and defender positions |
| Expected goals | Location, type, rebound and strength only (AUC 0.74) | Goalie position and whether he was set, screens, the pre-shot pass and how far it moved the goalie, shooter speed: the inputs that separate tracking-based shot models from location-only ones |
| Saves | One goal rate per goalie per danger tier, scaled | Save probability from the goalie's position, depth and movement relative to the shot path, per goalie |
| Systems and coverage | Formations drawn from references, the same for both teams except for the chosen system | Each team's observed spots by situation (forecheck, breakout, coverage), estimated as positional distributions, and coverage tuned to real defensive positioning, which is where the engine's biggest error lives |
| Play Review | Nine positions inferred per shot; pass and pressure numbers set by hand | All positions observed; completion and pressure fit from real passes; the replay is the real play |
| Validation | League aggregates and per-team rates | Situation-level agreement: load real game states, compare the engine's choice with the player's, and check strategy effects against real outcomes |

The architecture allows each swap without a new engine: kinematic parameters, systems and thresholds are data files, the outcome models are single functions (`xgModel`, the save roll), and the decision policy is one function per role (`carrierThink`, `assignTargets`, `formationSpot`) that a fitted model can replace behind the same interface. The lab's paired-seed method and the determinism carry over unchanged.
